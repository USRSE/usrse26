Welcome to USRSE'26, the fourth annual US Research Software Engineering conference, hosted by the US Research Software Engineer Association (US-RSE). We are thrilled to have you in San Jose. With this year's theme, "Advancing Science in the Age of AI," a practical question arises: how can research software engineers and their collaborators increase research impact during these rapidly changing times? AI is changing how software gets written, tested, and maintained. This is far from the whole picture, as it brings real hurdles, from code that is easier to generate than to understand, to new questions about correctness, credit, security, and sustainability. These tools should assist our work, not replace the people who do it. The craft of building reliable, reproducible software, and of supporting the people behind it, matters as much now as ever.

We’re thrilled about our program. With so many great sessions, choosing among them can be difficult. Across our parallel tracks you will find papers, talks, notebooks, workshops, Birds of a Feather sessions, posters, and our Rapid Access Microtalks, as well as multiple student and early career sessions and our inaugural job fair. AI topics run through much of the program, from retrieval assistants and agentic code modernization to guardrails and trust for AI-assisted development. Just as present are the foundations of our field: reproducible workflows, testing and sustainability, refactoring long-lived code, training and mentorship, user experience, HPC and cross-facility infrastructure, security, and honest reflections on RSE roles and careers. Whatever brought you here, you will find people who share your interests!

US-RSE is built and run by volunteers, and this conference exists only because so many of you gave your time to make it happen. Our planning committee, technical program committee and reviewers, keynote speakers, session chairs, panelists, mentors, early-career organizers, presenters, and on-site volunteers all deserve your thanks. When you meet them this week, please tell them so, and if you would like to help shape what comes next, reach out to a working group chair or a Steering Committee member. We are equally grateful to our sponsors, whose support underpins so much of what happens here and signals that research software, and the engineers who create it, are worth sustaining. Please take time to talk with their representatives during breaks and in the foyer.

This year we hope that you meet someone new, leave with an idea you can use, and return to your work with renewed energy that strengthens the RSE community for years to come.

Alex Koufos and Keith Beattie
*General Chairs, USRSE'26*
